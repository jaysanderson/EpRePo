SLC12A5, GRIA2/GluA2, HNRNPU, RARS2. I come to the portal to choose and dose treatment, check
contraindications (sodium-channel blockers in Dravet, lamotrigine, vigabatrin), confirm fenfluramine,

## 2. Verdict in five sentences

For the questions where the corpus genuinely holds the paper, this is the best grounded answer tool I
have used: the fenfluramine, cannabidiol-clobazam, STXBP1 natural history and SYNGAP1 seizure-type
answers were verbatim-correct to the percentage, and the honest declines on the PBS and rescue
midazolam questions are exactly what I want a junior to see. I would not yet trust it unsupervised
in a clinic room, because it told me vigabatrin is contraindicated in Dravet syndrome "as it is a
sodium channel blocking antiepileptic drug" with four citation markers behind it, and none of the
four papers says either thing - one pharmacological fabrication like that in a safety answer is one
too many. The safety-audit pointer that fixed the lamotrigine omission is real on the wire but the
web page throws it away when the final answer arrives, and several "Open PDF at page N" evidence
cards land on a bibliography entry rather than a finding, so the checking step a careful clinician
relies on is weaker than it looks. The videos play well and are correctly labelled by paper, but
half of them carry auto-summaries about "a worm on plain white paper", "living rooms" and "snakes",
and none of them can be found from the Search box, which for my use case is the whole point. I would
use it again this week for dosing and phenotype questions, keep the video library behind the Library
facet until the descriptions are rewritten, and not put it in front of a family or a registrar
until the vigabatrin class of error is caught by the audit.

## 8. Feature wishes

1. A "Videos" tab in Search, or media included in the search configuration, so I can type
   "eyelid myoclonia video" and get the SYNGAP1 clip (it is what I came for).
2. A drug-safety mode for the clinical intent that lists, per drug the answer names, the exact
   sentence and page it relies on, and refuses to call anything contraindicated without one.
3. A "what the corpus lacks" line in every clinical answer (like Q06's "not mentioned in the
   context") so trainees learn the boundary.
4. Per-syndrome landing pages (Dravet, SYNGAP1, STXBP1) that collect the consensus, the trials, the
   natural-history papers and the videos in one place instead of one search at a time.
5. Video cards that state species/patient, seizure type and duration, with "silent" flagged, and a
   still frame as the thumbnail.
6. A weight-based calculator next to any answer that gives mg/kg (fenfluramine, stiripentol, CBD),
   with the maximum daily dose applied automatically.
7. Evidence cards that quote a finding sentence, never a reference-list entry, and show the
   highlight or say it could not be found.
8. A session-aware rate limit with a visible "retrying in N s" so a clinic behind one IP is never
   shown "Too Many Requests".

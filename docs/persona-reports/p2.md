# EpRePo persona evaluation - P2, Dr Okafor (clinical geneticist and genetic counsellor)

## 1. Persona and session

**Who.** Dr Okafor, clinical geneticist and genetic counsellor in a metropolitan genetics service. I
run a weekly epilepsy genetics clinic with a paediatric neurologist and a monthly reproductive
genetics clinic. What I need from a research portal: interpret a variant against the published
phenotypic spectrum of its gene, give a defensible recurrence risk, know what a gene panel or exome
is likely to return before I consent a family, and know where common (polygenic) risk fits in the
non-monogenic epilepsies. Everything I say in a counselling room has to be traceable to a paper I
can put in front of the family or a colleague.

**What I set out to do.** Ask 13 questions in my own voice (including three the corpus should not be
able to answer), verify every cited number against the cited resource's text, run 12 searches
(bare gene symbols, phrases, a DOI, a nonsense query), browse the Library with facets and sort, open
and read resource pages, run six document-chat questions across three papers, and stress my
secondary surfaces: entity pages, the Knowledge graph, typeahead and suggest.

**Run.** 4 September 2026, about 2 h 40 min of wall clock against http://localhost:8787/t/eprepo.
Counts: **13 Ask questions** (plus 1 repeat with a forced intent), **6 document-chat questions** on 3
papers, **12 API searches** plus 4 in the browser, **8 route probes on the search surface**, **~20
resources opened** (metadata, extracted content or the reader), **10 entity pages**, the Knowledge
map at 1440 px and 390 px, and a headless visual pass over Ask, Search, Library, resource detail,
Graph and Entity in light and dark at 1440 px and 390 px.

## 2. Verdict in five sentences

The single-paper work is genuinely strong - document chat got six out of six of my questions right,
every number I checked was in the paper, and clicking an evidence card dropped me on page 6 of the
right PDF at the right figure, which is exactly what I need when I am building a variant
interpretation. The corpus-wide Ask is a different story: the answers read beautifully and are often
correct, but the citation layer is not trustworthy - one answer told me the diagnostic yield of
exome sequencing in DEEs is 75% when the cited paper says 75% of the cohort had *previously had*
exome sequencing, and several claims carry a marker pointing at a paper that does not contain them
(an SCN1A mosaicism figure attributed to a GNAO1 paper that never mentions SCN1A; a sodium-channel-
blocker drug list attributed to a testing-practice review that contains none of those drug names).
I cannot repeat a number from this portal to a family without opening the source myself, and if I
have to do that every time, the answer layer is a nice reading aid rather than a clinical tool.
The Knowledge graph, which should be my map of gene-phenotype space in a corpus with 414 genetic
studies, currently shows a random 120-node slice that changed completely between three page loads -
once SCN1A/Dravet, once JME and drug resistance, once amoxicillin and skin tests from a drug-allergy
paper - and every gene entity page tells me to "run the knowledge graph agent in Manage".
I would use it again tomorrow for search, Library and single-paper reading, which are already very
good; I would not put an Ask answer or the graph in front of a family, a trainee or a grant panel
until the citation mapping and the graph are fixed.

## 3. What is good

- **Document chat is accurate and fast.** Six questions on three papers, all six verified verbatim.
  On the GABRA3 paper: "The study involved 43 individuals with 19 GABRA3 variants ... 7 males and 13
  females" - the paper says "Among 43 individuals with 19 GABRA3 variants" and the GOF split matches.
  On the Scheffer 2023 exome paper: "35% of DEE patients, which equates to 36 out of 103 ...
  Management changes were made in 13 of these 36" and "patient 22 ... mother ... 10.5% mosaic ...
  quantified using droplet digital polymerase chain reaction" - all present in the text. Latency 4.7
  to 15.8 s to first token.
- **The evidence-card deep link works.** `/library/e470ace9...?page=6` opened the Oliver 2022
  EBioMedicine paper at page 6 of 11, showing Figure 2 "Familial epilepsy cases by decile of Epilepsy
  PRS" - the exact figure behind the cited passage. That is the single most useful thing here.
- **Bare gene symbols do the right thing.** `SCN1A`, `STXBP1`, `KCNQ2`, `PCDH19`, `SLC2A1` and
  `GRIN2A` all route to `lookup` / `portal-intent-lookup` by rule in 1-2 ms and return real documents
  (SCN1A 17 resources, STXBP1 12). The `Exact lookup - rule` chip explains why, which I like.
  Essay questions never routed to lookup - they went to general, review, data or clinical.
- **Retrieval finds the right papers.** `GLUT1 deficiency syndrome` returned the Klepper 2020
  international consensus statement first and the Suls 2008 SLC2A1 PED paper; `polygenic risk score`
  returned Ellis 2025, Oliver 2022 and Skotte 2022; `which genes cause generalised epilepsy in
  families` returned Ellis 2020 and Genes4Epilepsy. This is a well-chosen genetics corpus.
- **Two honest declines.** Preimplantation genetic testing for SYNGAP1: "This portal's content does
  not hold enough relevant material to answer this confidently" (refused: true, no invented protocol).
  ASO amenability for CHD2/GRIN2A: it correctly said the retrieved amenability paper covers SCN1A/
  SCN2A/SCN3A/SCN8A and "does not mention CHD2 or GRIN2A in relation to ASO therapy" - I verified
  that paper contains zero occurrences of CHD2 or GRIN2A. That is exactly the behaviour I want.
- **The GABRA3 and STXBP1 answers were correct end to end.** "GOF ... X-linked dominant ... LOF ...
  X-linked recessive ... epilepsy rarely observed ... females typically unaffected carriers" matches
  the J Clin Invest paper verbatim. STXBP1: 3.5 months median onset, 80% treatment-resistant, 3.2%
  mortality, median death 13 years, SUDEP 36% - every one verified in the two cited papers.
- **The clinical intent adds a safety line.** "Verify against current prescribing information before
  acting" appeared on both clinical-intent answers.
- **Resource pages are credible.** Real byline (Johannesen KM, Aung KP, Liao VW ...), journal,
  year, live DOI link (10.1172/JCI189830), topic chips, a working PDF reader with page controls, a
  summary, key takeaways, quotes of interest, and three sensible seeded chat questions ("How does
  GABRA3 variant inheritance differ between males and females?").
- **Library facets are honest and add up.** Articles 655 + Supplementary material 273 + Video and
  audio 53 = 981. Zero untagged resources, zero missing years, authors or journals across all 881
  catalogue rows I pulled, and no raw filenames - titles are real titles.
- **No horizontal overflow anywhere I measured**, at 1440 px or 390 px, light or dark; zero console
  errors across the whole headless pass.

## 6. Coverage notes

- **What the corpus holds for me, and it is good**: SCN1A (GoF spectrum, NDEEMA, methylation
  signature, mosaicism, prediction model), SCN2A (recurrent-variant functional correlates, DAPC,
  LoF mechanisms), KCNQ2 (Kv7.2 pore gating, R201C/R201H neonatal myoclonus, prompt-diagnosis
  outcomes), STXBP1 (adult natural history, early mortality, white-matter morphometry), PCDH19
  (271-individual meta-analysis, phenotypic-spectrum characterisation, multiomic NHR work), GRIN2A
  (epilepsy-aphasia, perisylvian anomalies), SLC2A1/GLUT1 (consensus statement, triheptanoin RCT,
  functional assay), GABRA3 (the 2026 J Clin Invest X-linked paper), CHD2 (episignature), plus
  exome/genome yield studies, Genes4Epilepsy, twin and polygenic-risk studies, and the ASO
  amenability paper. For a genetics clinic this is a serious collection.
- **What it does not hold, and how honestly the portal said so**: preimplantation genetic testing /
  reproductive technology protocols - **clean refusal**, no invented advice. ASO amenability for
  CHD2 or GRIN2A specifically - **honest**, and it correctly named which genes the amenability paper
  does cover. Formal penetrance estimates for DEPDC5 in familial focal epilepsy - it said "specific
  details about the penetrance ... are not directly provided", which is honest, but then padded the
  answer with two irrelevant cases and a leaked `(inference)` marker (P2-05).
- **Where honesty failed**: the exome-yield question (P2-01). The corpus holds the answer twice over
  and the portal instead reported a number that means something else, with full confidence and a
  citation. That is worse than a decline.
- **A gap I noticed as a counsellor**: nothing in the corpus gives empirical sibling recurrence risks
  for the common epilepsies (the twin and family studies are about liability, not counselling
  figures). The portal did not claim otherwise, which is right, but the recurrence-risk answer leaned
  entirely on parental mosaicism because that is all there is.

## 8. Feature wishes

1. **A gene dossier page** - one page per gene with its phenotypic spectrum, variant types, inheritance and every paper in the corpus, because that is the shape of my actual question and I currently assemble it from six searches.

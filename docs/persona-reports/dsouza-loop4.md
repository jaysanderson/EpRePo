## 6. The single change that would move the score most

**Make the replacement and removal steps prove the population and the quantity before they act, and refuse when they cannot.** Every P0 and four of the six P1s this loop come from the same new code path: the gate removes a sentence, looks for a substitute, and shows whatever paragraph shares an outcome noun. That path (1) kept a Dravet series' "25 of 205" for the VEM cohort and removed "2,709" because of a comma (D4-01), (2) kept Nashef's 5.9/1000 for Melbourne with a footnote instead of a removal (D4-02), (3) swapped a correct 12-month "64.2% (n = 4201)" for the ">12 months 29.5%" sentence (D4-03), (4) replaced an honest decline with an exposure quote from another paper (D4-04), and (5) carried the wrong cohort through a four-turn session under "High confidence". Concretely: before a substitute or a survivor is shown, require that its paragraph's population matches the question's named cohort (from the routing entities, not only from the sentence), that its figure and time point match the removed sentence's, and that the paper is the pinned one; if any check fails, show the decline ("the collection's paper on X reports Y; the figure you asked for is not stated") rather than a substitute. Normalise thousand separators, fractions and "n = " forms before deciding anything is unverifiable. Platform: pin the named study with `resource_filters` on the second prequery and audit against `rag_strategies: full_resource` text; generate with `answer_json_schema` carrying `{figure, timepoint, outcome, population, study, paragraph_id}` per statement so the substitute is chosen by figure and time point; bind markers from `citations: true` paragraph ids so a design sentence cannot carry an LGS paper's marker. The document chat proves the same platform answers all of these correctly when it is confined to one paper. This takes me from 6 to about 8.

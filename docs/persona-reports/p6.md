# P6 - Dr Chen, computational neuroscientist (seizure forecasting and ML)

## 1. Persona and session

**Who.** Dr Chen builds seizure forecasters. I came to EpRePo for multidien and circadian
seizure cycles, forecasting performance (AUC, sensitivity, time in warning), wearables and
implanted devices (NeuroVista, RNS), open datasets and their sizes, code and data availability,
and the supplementary data sheets behind the papers. I check every number before I would put it
on a slide.

**What I set out to do.** Ask the questions I would ask a new postdoc who had read the Melbourne
forecasting literature; search for the identifiers and phrases I use daily; browse the Library
for the forecasting corpus; chat with three papers; use the Supplementary data intent and the
Generate page; look at the UI at 1440 px and 390 px.

**Run.** 4 Sept 2026, roughly 12:00-13:10 local, against http://localhost:8787/t/eprepo shared
with nine other agents (eight HTTP 429s during the session, all retried).

**Counts.** 15 Ask questions on the portal-wide surface (13 distinct, 2 re-runs with a different
intent), 8 document-chat questions across 3 resources (23 asks in total), 10 searches, 1
briefing generated, 9 resources opened via API and 3 in the browser, 4 headless browser passes
(wide light, mobile light, wide "dark", one deep-link check). Every number in every answer was
grepped against `/resources/:id/content`; all working files are under
`scratchpad/persona-eval/p6/` (`q*.raw.sse`, `q*.done.txt`, `q*.summary.json`, `content/`,
`detail/`, `search/`, `shots/`).

## 2. Verdict in five sentences

For the questions the router sends to the evidence-review configuration, this is the best
grounded answer engine over the Melbourne forecasting literature I have used: every AUC,
sensitivity, hours-per-day and GitHub URL in Q2, Q3, Q7 and the document chats checked out
verbatim against the papers, and the passage deep link landed on the exact paragraph in the
paper. I would not yet trust it in front of a grant panel, because the same plain question
("What AUC and time in warning did the hybrid wearable forecasting approach achieve?") was
routed to the supplement-only `data` configuration in two of three runs and came back with an
AUC of 0.91 lifted from a genomic variant-pathogenicity appendix, with a citation marker,
under a "No direct evidence found" chip that did not remove the wrong number from the screen.
The "Latest evidence" answer invented 2025 publication years for 2021 and 2023 papers, and a
merchandised key takeaway on the Karoly heart-rate paper says 10 of 19 participants when the
paper says 17. The Library is mislabelled for my purposes (observational cohorts and simulation
studies carry a "Randomised Controlled Trial" chip, there is no forecasting topic, and the only
sorts are "added" date), and the Generate briefing was four paragraphs of platitudes I could not
show a lab meeting. I would use it again for review-style questions and document chat, with the
"verify against the paper" habit switched on, and I would send the routing and metadata bugs
back before letting a student near it.

## 6. Coverage notes

- The corpus is strong on the Melbourne / Mayo / King's forecasting programme: multiday
  cycles (Karoly 2021, Gregg 2023, Stirling 2025/2026, tetanus-toxin rat cycles), wearable
  forecasting (Nasseri 2025, Stirling 2021, 2023 EBioMedicine app pilot), NeuroVista-era
  intracranial work (Ung 2017 feature drift, Kiral-Kornek 2018, cloud detection 2019, LSTM
  2024), the EEG datasets review (Wong 2023), minimum clinical utility standards (2024), and
  the ILAE rhythms expert opinion (2026).
- Missing, and declined honestly: NeuroPace RNS forecasting studies (q12), any transformer
  work, statistical power methods (q13). The decline copy was clean in both cases.
- Missing, and NOT declined honestly: the same wearable AUC question (P6-01) and the LSTM
  metrics question (P6-04) were declared "not in the corpus" or answered from the wrong
  paper because of routing, although the papers are present.
- Data and code availability statements are often outside the extracted text (the
  `d6823179` extraction ends at "## Supplementary Material" with no availability section), so
  document chat cannot answer "is the code available?" for some papers even when the
  published article has a statement. Q7 nevertheless found three real repositories.
- Dark mode: this portal has no user theme toggle; "dark" is a tenant palette set in admin
  (`lib/theme.ts paletteMode`, palette `observatory`). Setting `data-theme=dark` had no
  effect (body stayed `rgb(255,255,255)`). I did not change tenant appearance (admin), so the
  dark pass is not covered by this persona.

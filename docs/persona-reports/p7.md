# Persona evaluation P7 - Prof Novak, basic neuroscience (rodent epileptogenesis)

## 1. Persona and session

**Who I am.** Prof Novak, a basic neuroscientist running a rodent epileptogenesis lab. I read
this corpus for animal models (kainate, pilocarpine, kindling, fluid percussion, genetic mouse
lines such as Scn1a and Hnrnpu), neuroinflammation, ketogenic mechanisms, glymphatic function and
- above all - **methods detail: species, strain, sex, dose, route and timing**. My hard rule is
that a rat result must never be presented as a human result, and a mouse strain must never be
guessed. I came to the portal to see whether I could use it to pull protocol parameters and
translational claims quickly enough to brief a PhD student, and safely enough to cite in a grant.

**Session.** 4 September 2026, roughly two and a half hours against

## 2. Verdict in five sentences

The portal is genuinely good at the thing I care most about - when it retrieves the right paper it
reports species, strain and dose accurately, and the document chat on a single paper was correct
every time I checked it against the PDF text, including a hard question that required separating
rat findings from mouse findings in the same study. But the citation layer, which is the whole
basis on which I would trust it, is broken: markers point at the wrong paper (a GAERS pharmacology
claim cited to a multi-omics paper that contains none of the drug names), markers point at nothing
at all in six of thirteen answers, and one answer gave me confident lateral fluid-percussion
parameters attributed to a human dementia trial protocol and a clinical trial amendment while the
same response also declared the corpus had nothing on the question. A ketogenic-diet answer
attributed a rodent dose-response claim to a paediatric saliva study with no rodent content in it,
and a glymphatic question about *models* was answered with human DTI-ALPS patient data while the
mouse glymphatic paper sat unused in its own retrieved sources - both are the exact species error
I said I would treat as a stop-the-line defect. I would use it again as a fast way to *find* the
right paper and then interrogate that paper directly, because that path works well; I would not
put an Ask answer in front of a grant panel or a student without opening every source myself, and
I would not use the Animal Models topic to survey the corpus because it holds 31 of what looks
like roughly a hundred animal papers. The Help assistant, which is meant to teach me how to use
all this, could not answer either of my two questions because the documentation is not indexed at
all, so a new user in my lab would be on their own.

## 3. What is good

- **Document chat is the strongest surface in the product.** On
  `/library/e41a3bcc...` (Cardiac alterations in rodent models of TLE) I asked "Which findings come
  from the mice and which from the rats? Be explicit about species for each result." It returned a
  clean split - KASE **Wistar rats**: cardiac fibrosis at subacute and chronic timepoints, reduced
  NaV1.5 and NCX1 in the septum, higher HW/BW ratio correlating with SE severity; SSSE **C57BL/6J
  mice**: no significant fibrosis, NaV1.5 down in right atrium subacutely, CaV3.2 and NCX1 up in
  left ventricle - and closed with "species-specific differences ... more pronounced structural
  changes in rats compared to mice". That is exactly the discipline I want.
- **Verified-correct method extraction.** Q1 (kainate dosing) returned "initial dose is typically
  5 mg/kg, followed by additional doses of 2.5 mg/kg ... up to 30 mg/kg", which matches
  `e41a3bcc` verbatim: "received 5 mg/kg i.p. KA ... another i.p. dose of 5 or 2.5 mg/kg was
  administered every 45 minutes up to a maximum of 30 mg/kg". Q9 (Z944) returned "Wistar rats ...
  only one out of seven Z944 treated animals reached the fully kindled state within 30
  stimulations", matching `37f3e6f3` word for word. Q2 (sodium selenate) gave 1 mg/kg/day
  subcutaneous infusion for four weeks in Wistar rats with a sustained reduction eight weeks after
  treatment - all four facts confirmed in `7ac9e290`.
- **Document chat cites its statistics.** Asked whether levetiracetam worked in the selenate
  study, it returned the actual ANOVA values - "no significant differences in seizure duration
  [F (2, 43)=0.5030, p=0.608] or severity [F (2, 43)=0.4563, p=0.636]" - which are in the paper.
- **Merchandising and MeSH keywords are excellent for my purposes.** Resource records carry
  `keywords: ['Rat', 'Kainic acid', 'Status Epilepticus', 'Animals', 'Rats', 'Rats, Wistar']` and
  `['Animals', 'Mice, Inbred C57BL', 'Mice', 'Rats', 'Rats, Wistar']`. Key takeaways name the
  species ("Sodium selenate reduced spontaneous seizures in rats"). That is more species metadata
  than most publisher sites give me.
- **Natural-language search beats keyword search here.** "how long does video-EEG monitoring last
  in the rat lateral fluid percussion model" returned the four EpiBioS4Rx harmonisation papers at
  0.89/0.84/0.74/0.72 - precisely right.
- **The Ask progress UI is credible.** Route chip ("General · classified · 70%") with a written
  rationale, a four-step timeline (Reading your question / Searching the corpus / Writing the
  answer / Checking the answer), Deep research, Stop and Export. It looks like an instrument, not
  a chatbot.
- **Honest refusal when it works.** Q13 (intrahippocampal kainate dose and coordinates in
  C57BL/6J) returned "The context does not provide specific information ..." rather than inventing
  a stereotaxic coordinate. That is the right answer and I was glad to see it.
- **Library list view and mobile.** At 390 px the list view shows real first-page PDF thumbnails,
  byline, journal, year, summary and topic chips with no horizontal overflow (measured
  `scrollWidth - clientWidth = 0` on every page I probed, wide and mobile). The 404 page is
  well-designed with jump links.
- **Search rate-limit error state** is written like a human wrote it: "You are asking faster than
  the portal can answer - please wait a moment and try again", with a Try again button.
- **The finished Ask answer is the best-looking screen in the product** (verified visually at
  1440 px and 390 px, screenshots `final-wide.png`, `final-mob.png`). "What did the Z944 amygdala
  kindling study find about seizure progression in Wistar rats?" returned a correct, well-set
  paragraph with the citation rendered as a proper superscript link (not raw `[1]` text), a route
  chip "General · classified · 80%" with its rationale, thumbs up/down, Compare configurations,
  verify/copy/retry/preview icons and a disclosure reading "Show retrieved sources" with the badge
  "**5 retrieved · 1 cited · 2011-2025**". That retrieved-versus-cited count is a genuinely good honesty signal.
  Measured `scrollWidth - clientWidth = 0` and zero overflowing elements at both widths.

## 8. Feature wishes

1. **A species and strain facet** built from the MeSH terms already stored (`Rats, Wistar`,
   `Mice, Inbred C57BL`, `Sheep`) - it is the single filter I would use most.
2. **A "preclinical only" toggle on Ask** that excludes human clinical sources entirely, so a
   model question can never be answered with patient data.
3. **A methods intent** that retrieves from Methods sections and STAR/key-resources tables, and
   returns a structured card: species, strain, sex, age, n, dose, route, timing, monitoring.
4. **Marker-level verification shown in the UI** - a tick when the cited passage actually contains
   the claim's numbers, because at the moment I have to grep the source myself to know.
5. **A model-comparison view**: pick kainate vs pilocarpine vs FPI vs kindling and see, side by
   side, what this corpus reports for each on epileptogenesis latency and drug response.
6. **Reference-list exclusion**, with a separate "cited by papers in this corpus but not held
   here" list - useful in itself, but it must never be mixed into the answer.
7. **Export a methods paragraph with citations** in a form I can paste into an ethics application
   or a grant, with the real published titles and DOIs.
8. **Show journal, year and study type on every citation chip**, so I can tell a 2009 slice study
   from a 2026 in vivo study without opening it.

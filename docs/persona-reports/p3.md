# Persona evaluation - p3: Prof Lindqvist, epilepsy neurosurgeon

## 1. Persona and session

**Who.** Prof Lindqvist, epilepsy neurosurgeon running a tertiary surgical program. Comes to the
portal to assemble MDT evidence: surgical candidacy, SEEG and intracranial EEG, seizure-freedom
after temporal lobectomy or lesionectomy, hippocampal sclerosis, focal cortical dysplasia,
neurostimulation (VNS, RNS, DBS), laser ablation, imaging for lesion detection, paediatric
hemispherotomy.

**What I set out to do.** Learn what the corpus holds for surgery, ask the questions I would
ask before an MDT, verify every number against the cited paper's text, build an investigation
(evidence file) from Ask answers and synthesise it, and follow "Open PDF at page N" from evidence
cards to see whether the highlight lands where the citation says.

**Run.** 4 Sept 2026, 12:20-13:20 local (about 60 minutes of API work plus four headless
browser passes totalling about 6 minutes). Shared server, ten agents on one IP.

**Counts.** 13 Ask questions (3 deliberately outside the corpus), 6 document-chat questions
across 3 resources, 10 searches (3 identifier, 2 gene/phrase, 3 multi-word phrases, 1 sentence,
1 nonsense), 8 resources opened via API and 4 in the browser, 1 investigation with 4 evidence
items and 1 synthesis, 11 PDF-reader probes.

**Corpus reality for this persona.** Stronger than the persona brief expected: the ATL 20-year
follow-up (McIntosh 2023), the 5-SENSE validation protocol, the MELD FCD atlas, the
cross-electrode RFTC review (2026), the SEEG Australian practice survey, an iEEG connectivity
meta-analysis (2026), ViEEG-from-MEG, HD-EEG+MEG source localisation, deep-tissue sequencing in
FCD, somatic Ras-MAPK in MTLE hippocampus, and VNS papers from 2021 to 2025 (CORE-VNS design,
baseline predictors, vagus physiology). Absent: RNS trials, ANT-DBS for epilepsy, LITT primary
data, hemispherotomy.

## 2. Verdict in five sentences

When the router picks the right configuration and the paper is in the corpus, this portal is
genuinely good: seven of my thirteen Ask answers were number-for-number correct against the
source text, the document chat on the ATL and RFTC papers was precise, the search page is fast
and handsome, and the investigation workflow (evidence file, tags, verdicts, synthesis with
numbered references, export to Word) is exactly the shape of an MDT preparation tool. I would
not, today, put an Ask answer in front of an MDT or a patient without opening the paper, because
three answers put real numbers under the wrong intervention with citation markers attached: a
brivaracetam registry's 8.0% seizure freedom was presented as the outcome of SEEG-guided
radiofrequency thermocoagulation, cross-electrode RFTC figures were presented as LITT and as
open temporal lobectomy, and the document chat quietly rewrote a confidence interval. The root
causes are identifiable rather than mysterious: the intent router sends "what rate / how
accurate" questions to a supplements-only configuration that never sees the primary paper, and
the review prompt lets the model paraphrase a review's comparison sentence into a per-procedure
claim. "Open PDF at page N" is off by one page, so the one affordance built to let me check a
claim in seconds sends me to the wrong page with no highlight, while the same link without a
page number finds and highlights the passage correctly. I would use it again for discovery,
library and document chat now, and for MDT evidence once the router, the page index and the
synthesis-note handling are fixed.

## 3. What is good

- **Grounded answers when retrieval is right.** Q1 (ATL 10 and 20-year seizure freedom): "36%
  ... at 10 years, and 32% at 20 years", "median follow-up of 22 years", "higher in patients
  with normal or distant lesions", "no significant difference ... year of surgery" - every claim
  present verbatim in 473163d3fa854a44a23e9325c17b13ee. Q2 (5-SENSE): all five components,
  83.3%/76.3%, cut-off 37.6 (SD 3.5), validation specificity 76% and sensitivity 52% - all in
  86ddc204094d4a949ee6bce89b03ba3a. Q11 (iEEG connectivity meta-analysis): OR 1.36, 1.39 vs
  1.35, 1.38 vs 1.30, 1.48 vs 1.24, 25 studies, 909 patients - all in
  87ad1815cf534e23ad55fdf414371718. Q10 (somatic Ras-MAPK, deep tissue sequencing): >40% of
  MTLE specimens, 28 patients, 71% yield, FCDIIb, "<1% of cells" - all verified.
- **Honest refusals for RNS and ANT-DBS.** Q4 ("RNS at two years ... vs anterior thalamic DBS")
  and Q12 ("randomised trial of ANT DBS in this corpus") both returned the clean refusal "This
  portal's content does not hold enough relevant material to answer this confidently" with
  `refused: true` and no citations. This is exactly right; the corpus holds VNS but not RNS or
  DBS-for-epilepsy trials, and the portal did not invent one.
- **Document chat is the most trustworthy surface.** Six questions on three papers; five were
  exact (log-rank p = .9 for year-of-surgery; 1.81-2.12x lesion size and 49/52 complications
  resolved; ten studies, 187 participants, 84% HH seizure freedom; 400 patients across 22
  centres; "up to 40% of patients are subsequently not operated"). Time to first token 4-6 s
  when the platform was not rate-limited.
- **Evidence cards carry the right honesty signals.** "reference list", "weak match" and
  "matched the summary" badges appear on the retrieved sources; the 27% "reference list / weak
  match" row for the Dynamical Network Models paper told me at a glance not to trust that
  citation. The "Low confidence" chip on the ViEEG answer was fair.
- **Investigation workflow.** Create, add evidence (with score, question, verdict, tags, note),
  filter by verdict, "Group by tag", "Synthesise the evidence" (8.4 s), synthesis rendered as
  Summary / Established by the evidence / Contested / Gaps / References with [n] resolved to
  the real paper titles, "Export to Word", "Ask this question", "Make current". Scoped to my
  client id (anonymous GET returned 404). This is the right product for MDT preparation.
- **Search page.** 0.3-0.7 s per query, sensible ranking for "hippocampal sclerosis",
  "DEPDC5", "stereo-EEG radiofrequency thermocoagulation" and "Engel class I"; the route chip
  explains the classification ("General · classified · 70%" with the rationale); the
  in-page AI answer's 429 error copy ("You are asking faster than the portal can answer - please
  wait a moment and try again") is human. DOI search resolves 10.1111/epi.17440 to the ATL
  paper at rank 1.
- **Resource page.** Real title, authors, journal, year, DOI link, summary, key takeaways
  ("51% of patients were seizure-free at 2 years post-surgery" - correct), quotes of interest,
  suggested questions, "You might also want" (neuropsychology in SEEG; cortical stimulation and
  language decline - both apt), PDF reader with page control. No horizontal overflow at 390 px
  on any page I measured (ask, search, library, resource, investigation, PDF reader).
- **The audit line.** Q6 ended with "Figures in this answer that do not appear in the cited
  passages: 44. Verify against the sources before relying on them." The instinct is right even
  though this instance was a false positive (see P3-13).

## 6. Coverage notes

- **Neurostimulation.** The persona brief expected only a 2016 review; the corpus actually holds VNS through 2025 (CORE-VNS design 2021, baseline predictors 2024, first-in-human vagus recordings 2024, vagus physiology 2025, a non-invasive neuromodulation SR protocol 2025). Q6 was answered well from those (7.1 failed ASMs, median age 24, 10.33 years from diagnosis, 34%/46% DeGiorgio figures all verified). For RNS and ANT-DBS the portal correctly declined (Q4, Q12): honest.
- **LITT.** No primary LITT study; the RFTC review quotes LITT figures second-hand. The portal did not say so and instead fabricated a comparison (P3-01): not honest.
- **Hemispherotomy.** Absent; the portal said so but dressed the refusal in four citations (P3-10): honest in words, misleading in form.
- **FCD surgery.** Present (MELD atlas, MELD detection, DEPDC5, deep tissue sequencing) but Q3 never reached it because of the data route (P3-04). Search "Which imaging modality best detects focal cortical dysplasia that is missed on conventional MRI?" did surface the MELD papers and the cortical morphometry review, so the corpus, not the search, is fine.
- **Temporal lobectomy outcomes, SEEG practice, 5-SENSE, iEEG connectivity, source localisation.** Present and answered accurately when routed to review/general.
- **API parameter names.** `GET /catalog?formatIds=article` (the brief's parameter name) is ignored; the real parameter is `format`. `labelsets=` on `/facets` is likewise `ls=`. Not user-facing.

## 8. Feature wishes

1. **"Add to evidence file" from a resource page selection** with the exact page and the highlighted PDF rectangle stored, so the MDT file carries a checkable location - the highlight machinery already exists.
2. **Per-procedure comparison table intent**: "ATL vs RFTC vs LITT: seizure freedom at 1, 2, 5 years, n, source" as a table with one citation per cell; a surgeon reads outcomes as a table, not prose.
3. **Study-design filter that is true** (RCT / cohort / case series / review / protocol) on Search and Library, because candidacy discussions hinge on evidence level.
4. **Outcome extraction on the resource page**: Engel/ILAE class, follow-up, n, aetiology as structured fields for surgical papers, so the corpus can be browsed as an outcomes registry.
5. **Synthesis that shows me what it excluded**: list the contradicted/not-relevant items it left out and the notes it read, so the brief is auditable before it goes to the MDT.
6. **Investigation export to a referenced document with real citations** (Vancouver style from DOI), since "Export to Word" is what leaves the portal and reaches colleagues.
7. **"Open PDF at the sentence"** for every number in an answer: click "36%" and land on the highlighted sentence - the single fastest way to earn a surgeon's trust.
8. **Corpus-boundary statement in the answer** ("the corpus holds 11 papers on SEEG, none on LITT primary outcomes") ahead of any comparison, so an absence is stated before a paraphrase can fill it.

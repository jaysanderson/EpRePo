# EpRePo persona evaluation - P5, Prof Delgado (neurotrauma, post-traumatic epilepsy)

## 1. Persona and session

**Who.** Prof Delgado, professor of neurotrauma. Runs a preclinical post-traumatic epilepsy (PTE)
programme and sits on a clinical TBI outcomes committee. Comes to the portal for: PTE incidence and
risk factors, biomarkers of epileptogenesis after TBI, common data elements and preclinical
harmonisation (EpiBioS4Rx-style multicentre work), prophylactic ASM evidence after TBI,
hospital-acquired infection as a PTE risk factor, and "what is newest in the field". Secondary
surfaces per the persona table: **Latest-evidence intent**, **Compare configurations**, **Sessions**.

**What I set out to do.** Reproduce a real morning of work: check the paediatric PTE incidence
figure before a grant paragraph; look up the effect size for hospital-acquired infection; check what
the EpiBioS4Rx harmonisation series actually reports; ask what is newest on PTE biomarkers; check
prophylaxis evidence; and see whether the portal admits what it does not hold.

**Run.** 4 Sept 2026, roughly 2 h 20 min against http://localhost:8787/t/eprepo (shared local
server, ten agents).

**Counts.** 16 Ask calls that produced answers (14 portal questions + 2 document-chat questions),
plus 4 further asks driven from the browser; 8 API searches + 3 UI searches; 5 resource pages opened
in the browser (4585e276 wide + dark, eb2948ba at 390 px, 92e3e17a via an evidence link, plus library
and search cards), 18 resource `/content` documents fetched and grepped for verification;
1 Compare-configurations run in the UI plus one API-level compare (general vs review);
8 headless browser passes (wide 1440, mobile 390 px). Raw streams, parsed answers and 20 screenshots
are in this directory (`q*.sse`, `dc*.sse`, `shots/`).

## 2. Verdict in five sentences

The corpus is genuinely excellent for my field - the whole EpiBioS4Rx harmonisation series, the
Victorian registry study on hospital-acquired infection, the paediatric incidence systematic review
and the 2025 preclinical CDE companions are all here - and when the portal retrieves the right paper
it is fast, well written and correct to the decimal (22% PTE prevalence, 7th-month video-EEG
definition, adjusted RR 1.59, 95% CI 1.11-2.28 all verified against full text). I would use it again
for orientation and for finding papers, and the document chat on a single PDF is the part I trust
most. I would **not** put an Ask answer in front of a grant panel or a student without opening every
cited PDF myself: the numbered markers routinely point at the wrong paper, markers run past the end
of the citation list ([8] and [9] where only 7 citations exist), and the Latest-evidence intent - the
one surface I was asked to lean on - gave me a wrong publication year attached to a citation and
described a paper by an author and journal that belong to a different paper. The Supplementary-data
intent is actively dangerous for my questions: it is triggered by the word "proportion" or
"incidence", can then only see supplementary files, and either produces a citation list reading
"Supplementary material 1" seven times or declares the portal has nothing, on a question the corpus
answers in its abstract. Fix the citation-to-source mapping, stop the data intent swallowing
quantitative questions, and this becomes a tool I would recommend to my group.

## 3. What is good

- **Coverage depth for PTE.** Searching `EpiBioS4Rx` returns 13 resources including every
  harmonisation paper (`ce28f908` FPI model production, `22326317` seizure detection, `53c9256d`
  HFOs, `c7616c56` plasma protein and miRNA, `92e0e3db` informatics tools, `9311df65` preface,
  `16e7a8f9` the 2024 outcome paper). That is the actual series, not a keyword coincidence.
- **The harmonisation answer (q4) is publication-grade.** "22% of the rats with lateral FPI-induced
  severe TBI developed epilepsy, defined as having at least one spontaneous seizure during the
  seventh month of video-EEG recording" - verified verbatim in `4585e276` ("In the present study,
  22% of the rats with lateral FPI-induced severe TBI developed epilepsy, that is, had at least one
  spontaneous seizure during the 7th month video-EEG recording"). The three sites, the CDE reporting
  and the "prevalence did not differ between centres" claim all check out.
- **Hospital-acquired infection answer (q2) is exactly right.** "20% compared to 9.2%" and
  "adjusted RR of 1.59 with a 95% CI of 1.11-2.28" match `eb2948ba` word for word.
- **Honest refusals.** Both deliberately unanswerable questions (blast injury in US veterans; a
  "2026 ILAE guideline" that does not exist) returned "This portal's content does not hold enough
  relevant material to answer this confidently" with no invented content. That is the right
  behaviour and it is the single biggest trust signal here.
- **Document chat.** On `4585e276`, "What proportion of rats developed epilepsy and how was epilepsy
  defined?" answered in 1.7 s to first token, correct and cited to the one document. The cohort
  question returned 100 (UEF) / 84 (Monash) / 61 (UCLA) and 121 MRI / 124 EEG - all verified.
- **Evidence links land.** "Open PDF at page 1" on an evidence card navigated to
  `/library/92e3e17a...?passage=...&page=1` and the exact passage was highlighted and scrolled into
  view in the extracted-text pane (`shots/pdf-at-page.png`). This is the feature I would use most.
- **Sessions work.** The ask trail persisted in the left rail across a navigation to Library and a
  full page reload, `GET /api/t/eprepo/sessions` returned it
  (`[{"id":"mtmcxula-2iu6yw","title":"Which risk factors for post-traumatic epilepsy were reported..."}]`),
  and clicking the card restored the full question and answer.
- **The Compare panel explains itself.** Each column prints its policy - "keyword + semantic - top 20
  - reranked - excl. supplement, media - +/-2 paragraphs - graph" vs "top 12 - ... - full text -
  synthesis prompt". That is the kind of transparency a technical buyer asks for.
- **Search is fast and sane.** 0.23-1.4 s, DOI lookup `10.1111/epi.17217` put the right paper first,
  a nonsense term returned a clean zero-result state, bylines and journals are correct.
- **Mobile is solid.** At a true 390 px layout viewport, Library, Search, a resource page (with the
  embedded PDF reader) and Ask all had `scrollWidth === innerWidth === 390`, no element overflowing,
  no clipped text.
- **"Ask next" follow-ups are on-topic and specific** ("What specific challenges did UCLA face
  compared to UEF and Monash in performing lateral FPI?").

## 5. Errors log

No HTTP 5xx, no SSE `error` events, no console errors (a browser pass listening for `console.error`
recorded an empty array), no blank screens, no stuck spinners other than the Search page's AI-answer
panel still legitimately streaming at screenshot time. Every failure was a 429.

```
POST /api/t/eprepo/route   -> 429  {"error":"rate_limited"}   (q3, q4, q7, q8, q9, q10 attempts)
POST /api/t/eprepo/ask     -> 429  {"error":"rate_limited"}   time_total 0.001 s
   e.g. q4  2026-09-04 ~12:35  body: {"error":"rate_limited"}
        q7  2026-09-04 ~12:52  body: {"error":"rate_limited"} (twice, cleared after ~35 s each)
UI  /t/eprepo/ask?ask=Which risk factors ... -> "Something went wrong / Too Many Requests / Retry"
UI  Compare configurations (general | review) -> both columns "Too Many Requests"
```

Ten agents share a 20-request-per-minute-per-IP bucket, so the 429 volume itself is an environment
artefact and I have recorded it as such. The **product** defects it exposed are real and are logged
as P5-09 (raw reason phrase in the customer-facing view, no retry in Compare) and P5-18 (shared-IP
keying). Server stdout (`scratchpad/server.log`) contained no errors at all during the session - only
`Listening on http://0.0.0.0:8787/` and an unrelated `[scheduler] auto-enrichment paused for frdc`.

## 6. Coverage notes

**What the corpus holds for my field, and holds well:** the complete EpiBioS4Rx preclinical
harmonisation series (2019 Epilepsy Research special issue plus the 2024 outcome papers), the 2024
Epilepsia paper on epilepsy phenotype reproducibility, the Victorian State Trauma Registry study on
hospital-acquired infection and PTE (2024), the paediatric PTE incidence protocol and its 2022
Epilepsia systematic review, the 2017 Epilepsia Open management review, neuroinflammation and
inflammasome reviews (2017, 2019, 2024), sodium selenate from rodent models through Phase II, the
2025 preclinical CDE companions (neuroimaging, omics), levetiracetam PK in the LFPI model, and 2025
plasma microRNA work. For post-traumatic epilepsy this is a better shelf than most institutional
libraries.

**What it does not hold, and how the portal behaved:** blast injury and US military/veteran cohorts -
declined honestly; ILAE or Brain Trauma Foundation guideline documents, including anything on
penetrating head injury - declined honestly. Both refusals were clean, with a useful next step
("try rephrasing ... or browsing the Library"). No hallucinated guideline, no invented veteran
cohort. That is the behaviour I wanted to see.

**The dishonest failure mode is different and worse:** it is not inventing content for absent topics,
it is mis-attributing content for *present* topics (P5-01, P5-02, P5-03) and refusing questions it
can answer when the `data` intent fires (P5-04). Coverage is not the problem here; citation binding
and intent routing are.

## 8. Feature wishes

1. **Make every `[n]` a verified link** - show the cited paper's title, year and the exact matched sentence on hover, because I check every number before I quote it.
2. **A "preclinical evidence" lens** - filter Ask and Search to animal work, with species, strain, sex, injury model and n on the card; I lose time separating rat FPI from human registry data.
3. **Cross-paper number extraction for a topic** - one table of PTE incidence by cohort (paediatric 10%, severe TBI 10-20%, rat FPI 22%, infection 20% vs 9.2%) with the source beside each.
4. **A CDE view** - the 2025 companion papers are here; let me browse their data elements as a checklist I can hand a new postdoc.
5. **Newest-first ordering with a visible date column on the Latest intent** - and never a year the cited paper does not carry.
6. **Exclude reference lists from retrieval by default, with a toggle** - most of the wrong answers I found came from bibliographies.
7. **Save a comparison** - Compare configurations produced the most useful insight of my session (review over-claims where general hedges); let me export that pair.
8. **A citation-export button per answer** - RIS or BibTeX for the cited set, so an Ask session can seed a grant reference list.

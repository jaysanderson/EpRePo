# Persona evaluation p8 - Dr Haddad, neuroimmunologist

## 1. Persona and session

**Who:** Dr Haddad, consultant neuroimmunologist running an autoimmune encephalitis (AE) clinic. Comes to the portal for anti-NMDAR and anti-LGI1/CASPR2 encephalitis, immunotherapy evidence (rituximab, IVIg, steroids, steroid-sparing agents), relapse rates, seizure outcomes after AE, Rasmussen encephalitis, and neuroinflammation biomarkers. Also wants a five-question registrar quiz (Assessment) and a way to be told when new AE papers land (Watches).

**Set out to do:** re-test the anti-NMDAR relapse question flagged as P1-9 in `docs/CLINICIAN-REVIEW-2.md` and verify the 28% figure against the paper; ask ten-plus clinical questions in my own words and check every number against the cited PDFs; search, browse and open the AE cluster of the library; chat with three papers; build a quiz; create and mark a watch; run a visual pass at 1440 px and 390 px.

**Run:** 4 Sept 2026, 12:20 to 12:52 AEST against `http://localhost:8787/t/eprepo` (981 resources), shared with nine other agents. All work under `scratchpad/persona-eval/p8/` (raw SSE streams in `asks/`, extracted text in `content/`, screenshots in `shots/`).

**Counts:** 11 portal questions (Ask, routed via `/route` then `/ask`; one re-run with an intent override) plus 6 document-chat questions across 3 papers = 17 answers verified; 8 API searches and 3 UI searches; 20 resources opened via the API and 1 resource page in the browser; 1 quiz generated via the API and 1 via the UI; 1 watch created, listed, marked seen and re-run; 1 help-docs question; 20 screenshots (Search, Library, Resource, Assessment, Ask at wide and 390 px).

## 2. Verdict in five sentences

For the questions my corpus actually covers, this portal is the first tool I have used that gets autoimmune encephalitis numbers right: the anti-NMDAR relapse answer (12 to 16% literature, 28% in the Australian cohort, HR 0.11, 89% risk reduction, protection to 6 but not 12 months) is verbatim in Seery 2025, and the review-2 flag P1-9 was the reviewer's misread, not the portal's. Document chat is precise enough to use at the desk (19 (28%) on maintenance IVIg, 58% vs 15% in relapsers, LON in 2 patients at <1.5 x 10^9/L beyond 4 weeks, hippocampal sclerosis in 2 (7%) of NMDAR patients - all correct). I would not yet trust it unsupervised in front of a registrar, because twice it refused questions the corpus answers plainly (rituximab dosing; NLR as a biomarker) while on the Rasmussen question it streamed a confident answer citing three papers that are not in the corpus and only then appended "no answer has been generated", and on anti-GABA-B it asserted "approximately half have SCLC" with citations to sources that never mention small cell lung cancer. The Assessment page cannot build a quiz on autoimmune encephalitis at all (eight fixed "Industry Knowledge Areas"), and the quiz it does build is undergraduate level, so it is not a registrar teaching tool yet. I would use it again for my own literature checks and I would show the resource page and document chat to a colleague today; the Ask surface needs the refusal path and the citation binder fixed before it goes in front of a patient or a grant panel.

## 3. What is good

- **P1-9 is closed, in the portal's favour.** Q1 "What is the relapse rate in anti-NMDAR encephalitis and does a single course of rituximab reduce it?" (Clinical decision, classifier 1.0) returned: "approximately 12%-16% ... although this study found a higher proportion of patients (28%) with a relapsing course ... reduces the risk of first relapse by 89% (HR 0.11, 95% CI 0.02-0.70, p = 0.02) ... last for about 6 months, but not at 12 months ... a six-monthly dosing schedule might be more effective". Every figure is in resource 7622042349d2421d80e15cbc0a568aa3: "A total of 19 patients (28%) had at least 1 relapse", "In our study, a higher proportion of patients (28%) had a relapsing course", "HR 0.11, 95% CI 0.02-0.70, p = 0.02", "6-monthly treatment effect ... 0.05 ... 12-monthly ... 0.60 0.15-2.44 p 0.47". Review 2 only found the IVIg 28%; the relapse 28% is on the same page.
- **Q9 (maintenance IVIg confounding) is the best answer of the session**: it reproduced the paper's own caution ("meta-analysis ... at least 6 months of therapy ... high proportion of individual case reports and small series, with only 24 patients receiving maintenance IVIg ... more frequently used in patients who relapsed ... potential confounding factor") and closed with "insufficient evidence to substantiate the efficacy or inefficacy". All verbatim in Seery 2025.
- **Q2 (LGI1 second-line and steroid-sparing)** correctly said steroids beat IVIg for FBDS, PLEX is an option, mycophenolate/azathioprine reduce relapse, and that both rituximab-treated and untreated patients improved on mRS. All present in 8565cc8f77a444608d6fded78f602c79.
- **Q3 (chronic epilepsy after AE)** gave "10% to 40% at two years", which is in the RESILIENCE protocol ("rates reported from 10% to 40% at 2 years after disease onset"), and honestly said "the context does not specify which antibody type carries the highest risk".
- **Document chat is exact.** Six of six answers verified word for word (section 7). Time to first token 4 to 11 s.
- **Resource page** (7622...): real title, first-author byline, journal, year, DOI, "View original source", 13-page PDF reader with page controls, three sensible suggested questions ("Does a single course of rituximab delay time to first relapse..."), summary and key takeaways that are correct (HR 0.11, 0.05, 0.60 all in the paper). This is the screen I would show a colleague.
- **Merchandising** of the AE cluster is credible: key takeaways on the LGI1 paper ("Relapses occur in 14%-35% of patients") and the NLR paper ("OR 1.32, 95% CI 1.03-1.69, p = 0.029") are in the papers.
- **Watches work end to end** (API and UI): POST returns the watch, the Search page shows "Watching", a "SAVED" chip with a remove button, and the helper line "Watched searches are re-checked daily - a dot appears here when results change"; the chip re-runs the search; the list survives navigation.
